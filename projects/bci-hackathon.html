BR41N.IO · 3rd place · 2026

BR41N.IO Brain-Computer Interface Hackathon

Lobes of Confusion (Group 35) tackled a stroke rehabilitation data analysis challenge at the BR41N.IO Brain-Computer Interface Designer’s Hackathon. The team earned third place in the BCI Data Analysis Projects division during the 2026 BCI & Neurotechnology Spring School organized by g.tec medical engineering.

BCIMotor imageryCSPLDASVMData analysis

My role & contributions

Team member alongside Nicholas Ross, Jonathan Pham, Tharan Suresh, and Aaron Gholston. The presentation documents the team’s shared work on motor imagery classification.

  • Worked on the stroke rehabilitation data analysis challenge under hackathon constraints.
  • The team compared common spatial patterns (CSP) with linear discriminant analysis (LDA) and support vector machine (SVM) classifiers.
  • The presentation explored timing windows, patient-specific classification performance, and the effect of signal artifacts.

Research & project context

The team concluded that optimal timing windows vary across patients and identified adaptive artifact rejection as a direction for further work. These are hackathon analysis findings, rather than demonstrated clinical outcomes.

Read the presentation summary

The project investigates motor imagery decoding for stroke rehabilitation, where brain-computer interfaces and functional electrical stimulation can support upper-limb rehabilitation. The team compared CSP + LDA and CSP + SVM classifiers with supplied baseline pipelines, examining performance before and after rehabilitation and across analysis windows.

The presentation emphasizes patient-specific timing windows and the impact of signal artifacts. The team proposes personalized BCI algorithms and adaptive artifact rejection as directions for future work. The slides contain the detailed accuracy comparisons and confusion matrices.
